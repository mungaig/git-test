### Question #3 response:

Hi Jamestk,

Thank you for your question. Looking into your account, we can verify that you have personal repository. Collaborators are able to fork personal repositories without needing permission from the owner. 

To prevent this from happening in future, you may want to create an organization account or to [convert your personal account to an organization](https://docs.github.com/en/account-and-profile/setting-up-and-managing-your-personal-account-on-github/managing-your-personal-account/converting-a-user-into-an-organization). By default, new organizations are configured to disallow the forking of private repositories. With an organization repository, you will also be able to allow or prevent forking of any private repository owned by your organization.

Unfortunately, you will not be able to [delete the the forked repo](https://github.com/github/docs/blob/main/content/account-and-profile/setting-up-and-managing-your-personal-account-on-github/managing-access-to-your-personal-repositories/removing-a-collaborator-from-a-personal-repository.md?form=MG0AV3). When it was forked, a separate copy of the repo was created under the collaborator's account and they have control over it. If you want, you could remove the collaborator's access to the original repository, which will prevent them from making further contributions. This will however not delete the forked repository.

You can learn more about forking permissions from [our docs here](https://docs.github.com/en/pull-requests/collaborating-with-pull-requests/working-with-forks/about-permissions-and-visibility-of-forks). We hope this answers your questions. Please let us know if you need more information regarding this.
